# Flippa Highlights — Partnership Term Sheet (DRAFT)

**Between:** Trail of Joy / Coached by Hooks ("**TOJ/Hooks**") and **Flippa Highlights**
("**Flippa**") — the Digital Asset Partner.

> ⚠️ **DRAFT · business terms only · not legal advice.** Money splits and every item
> touching a minor's NIL require attorney review in the governing state before use.
> See §5 — the legal gate is load-bearing.

---

## 1. What the partnership is

Flippa Highlights is the **digital-asset marketplace + download** for the highlights
and edits produced for the Coached by Hooks / STL roster. A highlight stops being a
post that scrolls away and becomes an **owned, listable, monetizable asset** —
priced and sold on [flippahighlights.com](https://www.flippahighlights.com/marketplace)
with [download](https://www.flippahighlights.com/download) delivery.

**Flippa is the storefront + source of truth for pricing.** What an edit or asset
costs appears on Flippa Highlights, not in a side quote.

## 2. What flows where

```
Subject Media films + edits (retainer)  →  asset listed on Flippa Highlights
   →  sells  →  revenue split (§3)  →  creator paid + athlete's NIL respected (§5)
```

- **Coverage** is paid by the monthly retainer ($3–5K/mo, 3 days/wk, 50–100 roster).
- **Edits + assets** are priced and sold on Flippa.
- **Turnaround:** **48 hours** on a **24-hour request buffer.**

## 3. Revenue split — per asset sale on Flippa Highlights

Named parties, from gross sale price:

| Party | Share | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| **Flippa Highlights** | **25%** | marketplace · storefront · download delivery |
| **Subject Medias** | **15%** | production — the capture and the edit |
| **Coached by Hooks / TOJ** | **10%** | operation — curating, listing, business layer |
| **Named parties subtotal** | **50%** | |
| **Remaining 50% — see §3a** | **50%** | *(athlete NIL share + / or creator split — needs one decision, see below)* |

### 3a. The remaining 50% — the one decision to make

The three named parties are **50%** in total. What the other **50%** is for is the
call to make now — because it changes both the athlete's NIL position and the
signed-creator's pay math from the earlier draft:

**Option A · All to athlete/family NIL (the clean version).**
The athlete keeps **50%** of every asset sale that features their likeness. This is
the strongest NIL position you can offer a family and the cleanest way to stay clear
of athlete-agent law — the athlete gets the largest single share of their own
likeness. **Signed creators** are then paid from **Subject Medias' 15%** production
share (e.g., 50% of that = 7.5% to the creator), not from a fourth top-line share.

**Option B · Split between athlete + creator.**
Cut the 50% into an athlete share (e.g., **35–40%**) and a creator share (e.g.,
**10–15%** for signed creators). Simpler on the creator side, smaller NIL share to
the athlete. Attorney should size the minimum athlete NIL share that keeps the
program compliant with state NIL/eligibility.

**Option C · Hybrid tiers.**
Different splits by athlete tier (Foundation/Build/Season/Elite) or by asset type.
More flexible, more complex.

> **Default until you confirm:** Option A — 50% to athlete/family NIL, creator paid
> from Subject Medias' share. It matches the "athlete owns the upside" line and
> keeps TOJ out of agency.

### 3b. What "50%" of the earlier creator draft becomes

The prior draft said signed creators get **50%** — that number was of an undefined
"net." With these splits, that language needs to say **50% of *which* pool.**
Under Option A, it's **50% of Subject Medias' 15%**. Under Option B, it's a defined
creator share out of the remaining 50%. The creator content agreement gets updated
to match once §3a is chosen.

## 4. Which assets / which athletes (tiers)

- **Roster athletes on active coverage** are eligible to have assets listed.

- Asset types: game highlights, feature edits, and downloadable packs from the same
  capture Subject Media already produces.

## 5. ⚠️ The legal gate — minor NIL + consent (read before listing anything)

Selling a highlight that features an athlete monetizes that athlete's **name, image,
and likeness.** For the many roster athletes who are minors, that pulls in:

1. **Guardian consent — required, per asset.** A minor's asset is listed only with
   guardian **`c4`** consent (the same public-roster/tradable-asset gate the recruiting
   funnel enforces). No consent → not listed. Consent is withdrawable; a delist +
   takedown request is honored (target 72h).
2. **State NIL + HS eligibility rules.** Monetizing a HS athlete's NIL is governed by
   the athlete's state statute and athletic-association rules, which vary widely and in
   some states restrict or bar it. **Counsel confirms it's permitted before a single
   minor's asset is monetized.**
3. **TOJ/Hooks stays out of agency.** TOJ/Hooks produce and list the content and
   operate the marketplace relationship — they do **not** act as the athlete's agent or
   negotiate NIL deals for a cut beyond the disclosed production/operation share. The
   athlete owns the likeness and their NIL share.
4. **Disclosure + tax.** NIL value to the athlete is disclosable and may be taxable.

**Default until counsel clears minor-athlete monetization:** list only assets that do
**not** feature a minor's identifiable likeness (team/action B-roll, adult creators'
work), or athletes who are 18+. The full minor-athlete asset program turns on only
after the §5 review.

## 8. Handling

Every asset that features an athlete runs behind the guardian-consent gate. No minor's
likeness is listed, sold, or downloaded without `c4` consent + the §5 clearance. This
term sheet is a draft; the partnership agreement + the creator agreement are the
executable instruments, both attorney-reviewed. Examples are illustrative — no real
athlete data here.
